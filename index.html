Building From First Principles

I didn't start in robotics by reading textbooks — I started by burning microcontrollers, tearing apart motors, and debugging broken code at 2 AM.

Currently studying in Class 10 at SOSE Shalimar Bagh, I focus on the intersection of Physical AI, Autonomous Navigation, and High-Speed Web Applications. I believe that true engineering excellence is proven when your code commands physical hardware in dynamic, unstructured environments.

The Builder's Mindset

“Flawless theory on paper means nothing until it survives a real-world test without crashing.”

I test edge cases aggressively. If a robot's obstacle avoidance algorithm hasn't been pushed to high-speed close-proximity obstacles, its safety rating is theoretical. I design systems with hardware-level fail-safes and robust error recovery routines.

ENGINEERING MANIFESTO

Core Philosophies.

Principles forged through broken prototypes, late-night debugging, and hackathon wins.

01
“Build first. Broken prototypes teach faster than flawless theory.”
I learn ten times more from a broken motor or failed API than from a textbook diagram that was never tested.
02
“Break things on purpose.”
If your system hasn't crashed under extreme load, you don't know where its true physical limits lie.
03
“Software alone isn't enough.”
The most captivating engineering happens right at the boundary where intelligent code commands physical actuators.
04
“Compete to evolve.”
Hackathons force you to synthesize architecture, eliminate bottlenecks, and ship under pressure.
